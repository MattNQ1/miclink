Privacy

Privacy Policy

What the MicLink apps and this website do with your data, in plain language.

Effective October 2026

The short version

  • Your microphone audio goes only from your iPhone to your own PC. It is never recorded, uploaded or used for ads.
  • There is no account and no MicLink server. We never receive your audio, and nothing the apps do tells us who you are.
  • The free plan of the iPhone app shows ads through Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, Google's ad software collects some device and usage data. MicLink Pro has no ads.
  • MicLink does not ask for permission to track you across other companies' apps and websites.
  • MicLink Pro subscriptions are handled by Apple. We never see your payment details.
  • MicLink for Windows collects nothing. This website sets no cookies and has no analytics.

Who this policy covers

MicLink is made by Matthew Malek ("we", "us"). This policy covers the MicLink app for iPhone, the MicLink app for Windows and this website.

Three other companies take part in what is described below, each under its own privacy policy: Google provides the ads in the free plan of the iPhone app, Apple runs the App Store and handles subscriptions, and GitHub hosts this website and the Windows download.

Your microphone and your audio

The iPhone app uses the microphone only while a session is running: from the moment you tap Start until you pause or disconnect. While MicLink is paused, the microphone is released and other apps can use it.

The audio is sent live to the MicLink app on your own PC, over the USB cable or your local network. It is not recorded, not stored and never sent to us or to anyone else. It does not travel over the internet.

Your audio is never given to the advertising software described below, and it is not used to choose ads. While an ad plays, MicLink sends silence to the PC, so the ad's sound does not reach your PC either.

On the PC, MicLink plays the audio into a virtual audio device so that other apps on your PC can use it as a microphone. What those apps do with your voice (for example a call or streaming app) is covered by their own privacy policies.

MicLink does not add encryption to the audio between your iPhone and your PC. Over Wi-Fi it crosses your local network as it is, and other devices on the same network could reach it while a session is running. Use MicLink on a network you trust, such as your home network.

Local network access

The iPhone app asks for permission to use your local network. It needs this so the MicLink app on your PC can find your iPhone and receive its audio over Wi-Fi. The Windows app looks for your iPhone on the local network and connects to it. Neither app uses this access to collect information about your network or about other devices.

Advertising in the free plan

The iPhone app is free to use: you get microphone time in exchange for watching a video ad. The ads come from Google AdMob and are shown by Google's advertising software (the Google Mobile Ads SDK), which is built into the iPhone app. An ad is shown only when you choose to watch one. So that one is ready when you want it, the app contacts Google when you open it on the free plan: first to find out whether a consent message is required where you are, and then, once ads may be requested, to load an ad.

What Google's ad software collects

According to Google's own description of the data its Mobile Ads SDK collects on iPhone, the ad software may collect:

  • Your IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of your device. This is an approximate area, not your exact position.
  • A device identifier, such as the device's advertising identifier or an identifier that is limited to this app or to this developer's apps. MicLink does not ask for the permission that would make Apple's advertising identifier available (see below).
  • Advertising data, such as the ads you have seen.
  • How you use the app and the ads, such as app launches, taps and video views.
  • Performance data, such as launch time, hang rate and energy use.
  • Crash logs and other diagnostic information.

Google says it uses this data to show ads (which includes sharing it with other companies that show ads through Google), to measure and improve how ads perform, for analytics, and to find problems and improve its software.

This data goes from the app straight to Google. We do not receive it and cannot look up what Google holds about a device. What we see in our AdMob account are totals, such as how many ads were shown in a country and what they earned.

What is never part of it

  • Your microphone audio.
  • Your name, email address or phone number. MicLink has no account and never asks for them.
  • Your contacts, photos or precise location. MicLink does not ask iOS for access to any of them.

No tracking across other companies' apps

MicLink does not show Apple's "Allow Tracking" prompt and does not ask for that permission. Without it, iOS does not give the ad software your iPhone's advertising identifier (IDFA), and Apple's rules do not allow an app to track you across apps and websites owned by other companies. Ads can still be chosen with information from MicLink itself, such as your approximate location and the ads you have already seen here.

Your consent and your choices

  • Consent message. Where privacy law requires consent or an opt-out, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the app shows a message from Google before it requests any ad. There you decide how your data may be used for ads. Where the law requires such a choice, you can change it later in the app: tap the three dots at the top right, then Ad privacy choices.
  • MicLink Pro. With an active MicLink Pro subscription, the app does not start the ad software and does not request or show ads.
  • Google's own controls. Google explains how it uses information from apps that use its services, and the controls it offers, on its page How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. Its general policy is the Google Privacy Policy.

Google decides how long it keeps the data it collects and is responsible for it under its own policy. To see, correct or delete data that Google holds, use the tools and contact routes in Google's privacy policy.

MicLink Pro subscriptions

MicLink Pro is bought inside the iPhone app with Apple's in-app purchase. Apple takes the payment. We never receive your name, your email address, your card or any other payment detail.

To know whether Pro is active, the app asks iOS whether your Apple Account has a MicLink Pro subscription. The answer (which plan, and until when) is read on your iPhone and is not sent to us.

Apple gives us sales reports with totals, such as how many subscriptions were bought in a country and what they earned. They do not identify you. How Apple handles your purchase is described in the Apple Privacy Policy.

What stays on your devices

  • On the iPhone: your MicLink settings, such as the connection mode and the gain level, and how much free microphone time you have left. Google's ad software also keeps its own data on the iPhone, such as your consent choice.
  • On the PC: your MicLink settings and a small log file with technical messages (for example connection status and local network addresses) that helps when something goes wrong.

MicLink's own settings and the log stay on your devices and are never sent to us. Removing the iPhone app removes what it stored, including unused free time.

What we receive

From the apps: nothing that identifies you. MicLink has no account or sign-in, no analytics code of its own and no server run by us, so the apps have nowhere to send anything to us.

What reaches us are totals from the two companies mentioned above: ad statistics from Google AdMob, and download, subscription and (if you have agreed in your iPhone settings to share analytics with app developers) anonymous crash and usage statistics from Apple. We cannot identify you from them.

MicLink for Windows

The Windows app collects nothing. It has no ads, no account, no subscription and no analytics, and it does not contact us. It talks only to your iPhone.

Getting it set up involves downloads from other companies, which see your IP address as with any download:

  • The installer is hosted on GitHub.
  • VB-CABLE, the virtual audio device MicLink needs, is downloaded from VB-Audio Software during setup.
  • Apple's USB support for iPhone, if you choose cable support, is installed through Windows Package Manager, a part of Windows made by Microsoft, which downloads it from Apple. If Windows Package Manager is not available, MicLink opens the Microsoft Store page of Apple's "Apple Devices" app instead.

MicLink sends these companies nothing about you.

This website

This website sets no cookies, has no analytics and loads no fonts, scripts or images from other sites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log the IP addresses of visitors for security and operations, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement. We do not receive those logs.

If you contact us

If you write to us for help, we receive what you send, such as your email address and your message, and use it only to answer you. Problem reports posted on GitHub are public.

Children

MicLink is a tool for a general audience. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly let personal data of children be collected through it. Because the free plan shows ads, a child should use MicLink only with the agreement of a parent or guardian.

Your choices and rights

  • You can take away MicLink's microphone or local network permission at any time in the iPhone's Settings app.
  • You can decide how your data may be used for ads in Google's message, where the law gives you that choice, or use MicLink Pro, which has no ads.
  • You can remove the apps whenever you like.

We hold no personal data about you from the apps, so there is nothing for us to show, correct or delete. For data collected through ads, turn to Google; for your purchases, to Apple. Both are linked above. If you are unsure where to turn, write to us and we will point you to the right place.

Changes to this policy

If the apps or this policy change, we will update this page and the date at the top.
